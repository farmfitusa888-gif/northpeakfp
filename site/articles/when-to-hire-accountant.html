Working With an Accountant

When Should a Small Business Hire an Accountant?

Plenty of owners handle their own books at first — and should. But there's a point where doing it yourself costs more than it saves. Here's how to spot it.

By Chaudhry Ahmad, NorthPeak Financial Partners5 min read

In this guide

  1. You're Spending Hours on Books Instead of the Business
  2. Your Taxes Got Complicated
  3. You're Making Big Decisions
  4. Bookkeeper vs. Accountant
  5. The Real Return

01You're Spending Hours on Books Instead of the Business

If bookkeeping is eating the time you'd otherwise spend earning, the math has already tipped. Your hourly value in the business usually exceeds an accountant's fee.

02Your Taxes Got Complicated

Adding employees, selling in multiple states, or restructuring the business all add complexity that's easy to get wrong and expensive to fix.

03You're Making Big Decisions

Buying equipment, taking a loan, or changing structure all have tax consequences. A quick professional read before you act can save far more than it costs.

04Bookkeeper vs. Accountant

A bookkeeper records transactions while an accountant interprets them, handles your filings, and helps with planning and strategy. Match the level of help to what your business actually needs.

05The Real Return

The goal isn't just a filed return — it's fewer mistakes, less stress, and tax strategy you'd never spot alone. That's where the value shows up.
