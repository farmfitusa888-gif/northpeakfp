Sales Tax for Small Businesses: When You Owe It and How to Collect

Sales tax used to be simple: you collected it where you had a store. Online selling changed everything. Here's what applies now.

By Chaudhry Ahmad, NorthPeak Financial Partners6 min read

In this guide

  1. What Sales Tax Nexus Means
  2. Economic Nexus and Online Sales
  3. Registering to Collect
  4. Filing and Remitting
  5. Getting Help Early

01What Sales Tax Nexus Means

Nexus is the connection that requires you to collect sales tax in a state. It's created by physical presence — an office, inventory, employees — or, increasingly, by economic activity.

02Economic Nexus and Online Sales

After a landmark Supreme Court decision, states can require out-of-state sellers to collect tax once they cross a sales threshold in that state. If you sell online, you may owe tax in states you've never set foot in.

03Registering to Collect

Before collecting, you register with each state's tax authority. Collecting without registering — or registering and not filing — both cause problems.

04Filing and Remitting

You collect the tax, hold it, and remit it on the state's schedule. It's not your money; treat it as pass-through you're holding in trust.

05Getting Help Early

Multi-state sales tax gets complex fast. Setting up a system early, ideally with professional help, prevents a painful cleanup later.
