Quarterly Estimated Taxes: A Simple Guide for the Self-Employed

When you're self-employed, no one withholds taxes for you. The IRS expects you to pay as you go — four times a year. Here's how to stay ahead of it.

By Chaudhry Ahmad, NorthPeak Financial Partners5 min read

In this guide

  1. Why You Have to Pay Quarterly
  2. The Four Due Dates
  3. How to Estimate the Amount
  4. The Safe-Harbor Rule
  5. Avoiding the Penalty

01Why You Have to Pay Quarterly

Employees have taxes withheld from every paycheck. When you work for yourself, that job falls to you. The IRS wants its share throughout the year, not just in April.

02The Four Due Dates

Estimated payments are generally due in April, June, September, and January of the following year. Mark them on a calendar — the deadlines don't move to suit your cash flow. The IRS estimated taxes page lists the exact dates each year.

03How to Estimate the Amount

A common approach is to set aside a percentage of every payment you receive — often 25–30% — into a separate account. A safer method is the safe-harbor rule below.

04The Safe-Harbor Rule

If you pay at least 100% of last year's tax liability (110% for higher earners), you generally avoid the underpayment penalty even if you owe more at filing. It's the simplest way to stay protected.

05Avoiding the Penalty

Underpaying triggers an interest-based penalty. Paying on time and using safe harbor keeps you clear. A quick mid-year check-in helps you adjust before it's too late.
